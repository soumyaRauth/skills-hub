# Example: staying quiet, and a two-line consult

Engagement is earned by the request, not by the page it touches.

## A heading typo on a page with real SEO problems

The portfolio fixture, before any SEO work. Its homepage has a weak title,
no description and an about page hidden by `noindex`.

```
You:   Fix the typo 'Portfollio' in the homepage heading.

Agent: Fixed: index.html line 13, <h1>Portfollio</h1> → <h1>Portfolio</h1>.
```

No ⚡ line, no audit, no records, no mention of the `noindex`. The person
asked for a typo fix. Even "you could put your name in that heading instead"
is unrequested advice here. If they want SEO help, they will ask for it, and
the findings will still be there.

## Records exist, the request is not about search

A repository with `.seo-growth/` records. The request is to make the contact
form send a copy to the sender. Nothing about it touches search. The records
are not read, and nothing is announced.

## A change that can cost search traffic: two lines, then the work

A site whose records show its articles are its main source of visits.

```
You:   Change the blog URLs from /blog/my-post.html to /blog/my-post/.

Agent: ⚡ SEO Growth Engine — URL change on pages that receive search traffic
       Every old URL needs a permanent redirect to its new one, and internal links and the
       sitemap should point at the new URLs, or search engines will treat these as new pages.
       Making the change with those redirects (in _redirects), links and sitemap updates.
```

Then the change is made. The owner's request is the approval for the URL
change; the redirects, links and sitemap are part of doing it safely, not
extra scope. The consult changed what got built (the redirects exist), and
it took two lines. It is `CONSULT`, not `GATING`: the skill
does not block the change. It makes sure the change does not quietly drop
the pages from search.
